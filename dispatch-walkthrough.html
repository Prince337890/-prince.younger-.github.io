Scene 01 · Find the Load

A load is only worth what the math says it's worth.

Tuesday, 07:42. Your truck empties out in Dallas this morning. You're working the board before the driver finishes his coffee.

Load board · Dallas, TX · Dry van 53′
Dallas, TX → Memphis, TN$1,150 452 mi · posted 22 min ago
Dallas, TX → Atlanta, GA$2,100 780 mi · dry van · Apex Freight Group · posted 6 min ago
Dallas, TX → Oklahoma City, OK$620 206 mi · posted 41 min ago

Loaded miles 780 + deadhead to the shipper 40 = 820 all miles

$2,100 ÷ 820 mi = $2.56 True RPM

Below the $2.75 floor  Good lane, weak number. Worth a call — never worth the posted rate.

The math True RPM = rate ÷ ALL miles — deadhead included. The board shows $2,100 over 780 loaded miles and calls it $2.69. Your truck turns 820 miles to earn it. Rookies who quote off loaded miles only end up booking losers.

Scene 02 · Vet the Broker

Before you dial, make sure they can pay.

Two minutes of checking beats thirty days of chasing a broker who was never going to pay. Run Apex Freight Group before the call.

Broker check · Apex Freight Group · MC-987654
✓ Broker authority ACTIVE since 2016FMCSA licensing — an MC with history, not a pop-up
✓ $75K surety bond on filethe FMCSA-required bond that backs your invoice if they default
✓ Credit score 97 · pays in ~28 daysstrong credit, predictable days-to-pay
✓ Not on the FMCSA suspended listhaul for a suspended broker and you simply don't get paid
✓ Approved by the carrier's factoring companythe factor has to be willing to buy this broker's invoices

When you walk away — the counterexample

  • MC authority is 60 days old with zero credit history
  • Email domain doesn't match the company's website
  • "Truck must move TONIGHT — we'll quick-pay off the books"
  • They want your carrier to re-broker or double-broker the freight

Any one of these: pass. A load that never pays is worse than an empty truck.

Scene 04 · Setup Packet & Rate Confirmation

Nothing moves until the paper is signed.

New broker, so their portal needs the carrier's packet first. Your document vault turns a 30-minute setup into 3 minutes — then the RateCon lands in your inbox.

Carrier–broker setup · one time per broker
W-9 Certificate of Insurance MC Authority letter Factoring NOA

The NOA (Notice of Assignment) tells Apex to pay the carrier's factoring company — not the carrier — when the load delivers. Speed here wins loads: the truck that's set up first gets the freight.

The paperwork The RateCon is the contract — audit the rate, stops, appointment times, weight, and payment terms line by line before signing. The LPOA (Limited Power of Attorney) in your dispatch agreement authorizes exactly one thing: signing RateCons on the carrier's behalf.

NO POD = NO PAYMENT. The signed BOL is now the Proof of Delivery — the one document the broker, the factor, and the carrier all pay against. The driver photographs it before leaving the dock. Every load, every time.

Scene 08 · Paperwork Package & Invoice

Assemble the packet. Pick how fast you want the money.

Same afternoon — money doesn't move until the packet is complete and clean.

Signed RateCon Signed POD Lumper / detention receipts → Invoice packet · Load #A-58291 · $2,350

Path A — Invoice the broker direct

Keep 100%. Wait ~30 days.

Send Apex the invoice with the packet attached, on their Net 30 terms. No fee — but the carrier floats fuel, insurance, and payroll for a month while the check crawls.

InvoicedPaid · day ~30

Path B — Submit to factoring

Paid ~next business day. ~3% fee.

Submit the same packet to the factoring company. They advance the carrier the money — minus about 3% (~$70 here) — and collect from Apex themselves. Fuel doesn't wait 30 days; most owner-operators run this way.

SubmittedFunded · next day

The paperwork Lumper receipts (paid unloading crews) and detention (waiting past the free 2 hours) get billed back to the broker — but only if the receipts and timestamps are in the packet. No receipt, no reimbursement.
